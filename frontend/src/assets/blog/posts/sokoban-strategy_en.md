# Sokoban: From Beginner to Master

Sokoban is a classic puzzle game that has challenged players since the early 1980s. The premise is simple: you are a warehouse keeper, and you must push boxes onto designated target squares. However, the catch is that you can only *push* boxes, never pull them, and you cannot push more than one box at a time.

## 1. The Golden Rule: Avoid Corners and Edges
The most common way players fail a Sokoban level is by pushing a box into a corner. Since you cannot pull a box, a box in a corner is permanently stuck unless the target itself happens to be in that corner. 
Similarly, pushing a box against a wall can be dangerous. If there are no target squares along that wall, the box may be permanently trapped along that edge.

## 2. Work Backwards
Sometimes, the best way to solve a complex level is to start from the end. Look at the target squares and ask yourself: "Where does the last box need to come from to land here?" By tracing the path backwards, you can often identify choke points and figure out which boxes need to be moved first.

## 3. Keep Pathways Open
Sokoban is as much about managing your own mobility as it is about moving boxes. If you block a narrow corridor with a box, you might trap yourself or cut off access to another part of the puzzle. Always ensure you have an "escape route" before committing to a push.

## 4. The 'L' Shape Trap
Beware of grouping boxes together. Four boxes arranged in a 2x2 square are immovable. Even three boxes forming an 'L' shape against a wall or in an open area can become extremely difficult, if not impossible, to disentangle. Try to keep boxes separated until they are near their targets.

## 5. Practice and Patience
Ultimately, Sokoban requires spatial reasoning and patience. Don't be afraid to use the "Undo" button (if available) to test different theories. Every mistake is a learning opportunity.
