# The Hidden Logic of Minesweeper

Many casual players treat Minesweeper as a game of luck, clicking randomly until they inevitably hit a mine. However, beneath its simple exterior lies a game of pure logic and probability. Here is a guide to help you uncover the hidden logic of Minesweeper and win consistently.

## 1. Understanding the Numbers
The core of Minesweeper is simple: the number on a square tells you exactly how many mines are adjacent to it (horizontally, vertically, or diagonally). Therefore, a "1" means there is exactly one mine touching that square, a "2" means two mines, and so on.

## 2. The 1-1 Pattern
One of the most common and useful patterns is the "1-1 pattern" on a flat wall. If you have two "1"s next to each other along a straight edge of unrevealed squares, the third square out from the edge is always safe. This is because the two "1"s share the same two possible mine locations, meaning the mine must be in one of those two spots, leaving the adjacent spots safe.

## 3. The 1-2-1 Pattern
When you see a sequence of 1-2-1 against a wall of unrevealed squares, the mines are always located directly opposite the two "1"s. The square opposite the "2" is always safe. This pattern is incredibly reliable and appears frequently in almost every game.

## 4. The 1-2-2-1 Pattern
Similar to the 1-2-1, the 1-2-2-1 pattern dictates that the mines are located opposite the two "2"s, while the squares opposite the "1"s are safe. Recognizing these patterns quickly will drastically speed up your gameplay.

## 5. When to Guess
Despite your best logical efforts, there are situations in Minesweeper where you must guess. This usually happens at the very end of a game or when you are forced into a 50/50 situation. When guessing, try to make the guess that, if correct, will give you the most information to solve the rest of the board.
