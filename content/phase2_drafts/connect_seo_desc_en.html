About Connect Colors

Connect Colors is a relaxing yet brain-teasing logic puzzle where you pair matching color dots by drawing continuous lines across the grid. Each level asks you to connect every pair of identical colors while filling every single empty cell — no line may cross or overlap another. It is the perfect game to unwind, train your spatial reasoning, and sharpen your planning skills at any age.

Basic Rules & How to Play

The objective of Connect is simple:

  • Pair the Colors: Drag from one color dot to its matching partner to draw a pipe between them.
  • No Crossing: Lines cannot cross or overlap each other, so you must plan your routes carefully.
  • Fill the Board: Every empty square on the board must be covered by a line to complete the puzzle.

When all pairs are connected and every cell is filled, the level is solved and you advance to the next, more challenging grid.

Game Modes

  • Single Player: Play at your own pace through dozens of hand-crafted levels, from small 5x5 starter grids to demanding 12x12 master puzzles.
  • Speed PK: Race against another player to solve the same board first. Faster thinking and quicker planning win the match.

Difficulty Levels

Connect offers five difficulty tiers that scale the grid size and the number of color pairs:

  • Easy: 5x5 to 6x6 grids — perfect for beginners and younger players.
  • Medium: 7x7 to 8x8 grids with more pairs.
  • Hard: 9x9 to 10x10 grids that require careful route planning.
  • Expert: 11x11 to 12x12 grids that test your spatial reasoning to the limit.

Winning Strategies & Tips

  • Start from the edges: Dots near the border often have only one possible path. Connect them first to lock in the outer ring of the board.
  • Keep lines short: Shorter pipes leave more room for other pairs. If a pair can be connected by a tight route, do it early.
  • Avoid dead zones: Before drawing a long line, imagine where the remaining pairs will go. A line that isolates a dot makes the puzzle unsolvable.
  • Look for bottlenecks: When two colors sit close together with few free cells around them, treat them as a priority pair.
  • Use symmetry: Many levels are partially symmetric. Recognizing symmetrical layouts lets you mirror a solved region to the other side.
